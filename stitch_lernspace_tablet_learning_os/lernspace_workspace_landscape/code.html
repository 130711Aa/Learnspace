SECTION 4.3 — INDUCTION MECHANISMS COLUMBIA MEDICAL PRESS · 2024

NMDA Receptor-Dependent Long-Term Potentiation

The induction of long-term potentiation (LTP) at the Schaffer collateral–CA1 pyramidal cell synapse relies on the postsynaptic entry of calcium ions (Ca2+). Under resting physiological potentials, the channel pore of the N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor is electrostatically blocked by extracellular Mg2+ ions. This ionic impediment prevents current passage even during extensive glutamate binding.

Relief of this magnesium block necessitates substantial, synchronous depolarization of the postsynaptic dendritic spine. When high-frequency afferent stimulation drives non-NMDA ionotropic receptors—specifically α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors—the resultant influx of sodium (Na+) shifts the local membrane potential toward zero.
